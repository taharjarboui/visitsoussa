## A fortress for soldier-monks

Completed in 821 under the Aghlabid emir Ziyadat Allah I, the ribat of Sousse belonged to a chain of small forts watching over the coast. The *murabitun* who lived here divided their time between prayer and guard duty. The plan is strikingly plain: a square courtyard, cells on two levels, a vaulted prayer hall upstairs, and a 27-metre round watchtower added in 859.

## What to see

- The **entrance gate** and its vestibule, the earliest known rib vault in North Africa.
- The **prayer hall**, one of the oldest in the Maghreb.
- The **top of the tower**, up a narrow staircase: the medina, the Great Mosque right below, the harbour and the sea.

## Tip

Come in the late afternoon, when the stone turns honey-coloured and the light grazes the ramparts.
